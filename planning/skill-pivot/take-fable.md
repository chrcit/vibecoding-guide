# A first-principles take

## The shape

The best version has no lessons to complete. The learner brings a piece of their own work, the agent does it with them, and the skill watches for the moment a concept has just happened in their thread. Then it says so in two sentences and offers one thing to try.

The journey already has the right rule: every chapter lets the reader break the thing it teaches. On the site the break is a script over a made-up club list. In the tool it is the learner's own job, with the check really removed.

## The loop

1. You ask for something you need.
2. The agent does it.
3. The tutor points at one thing that just happened: it said "fixed" without running anything, the window is two-thirds full, you sat for six minutes watching.
4. It names the concept and suggests one move: "ask it to prove it", "start a second thread while this runs".
5. You try it, and one line goes into a notebook: what you tried and what changed.

At most one catch per turn, at its end, and "not now" is always accepted. A tutor that interrupts work gets uninstalled.

Skills are static and a new thread starts with an empty window, so the notebook is the only reason the tutor knows you next time. The course runs on the mechanism it teaches in "Write it down"; say so in session two, when the tutor picks up where they left off. Keep it outside any repository, so it follows the learner across projects and worktrees and is never committed with work data in it.

Concepts arrive in whatever order the work produces them, so the skill needs a dependency map where the site has a sequence. A concept is eligible once its prerequisites are in the notebook and the session has just produced an instance. If none comes, the tutor proposes the move: "this task splits in two; want to try a helper?"

## Session one

Fifteen minutes to something of theirs that works. No syllabus on screen, no menu of examples. The tutor asks what they work on and what they would like done this week, and offers examples only if they have nothing. The first catch is the smallest true one: you sent one message, it took fourteen steps, here they are. They leave with a working thing, one concept seen on their own work, three lines in a notebook and one move for next time. The feeling to aim for is "I understood what just happened to my files".

## Session ten

The tutor is close to silent. It stops commenting on anything the learner now does unprompted; twice without a nudge is a fair bar. The learner runs several threads and calls the skill to look back: what happened this week, where it went badly, what it cost. That is the owner's list of "use" skills, so the lessons and the tools are one thing at two ages. A lesson is a look-back with the explanation attached. By session ten the explanation has gone and the instrument stays.

## What a site cannot do

- Use the learner's own work as the example, including their own failure.
- Show numbers counted from their session where the site has to print "example".
- Answer the question they have, at their level, without a level switch.
- Let nested agents confuse for real: child threads pile up in the sidebar and the learner loses track.
- Make cost felt, because the meter is their plan.

Two limits belong beside that list. A live agent does not fail on cue: take the check away and it may still get the job right. Concepts that need a guaranteed failure need a planted-flaw exercise in a scratch project. And tutoring spends the learner's tokens and fills the window it is teaching them to keep clear.

## Where the plan becomes a pile

The brief holds about nine things. I would cut three down.

**Inline showcases.** Rebuilding the journey's animations in the chat gives you the website in a 728-pixel box. Rule: an inline page is drawn from this session's data, or it is a link to the site.

**Six "use" skills.** They are one question, "what happened here?", asked from different distances. Ship one look-back skill that writes one session file in a fixed shape, and one viewer page that reads it. "Visualize" is that viewer. "Something agents can analyse later" is that file.

**The knowledge base.** Left open it becomes a notes product. Keep it to the one notebook in a fixed shape.

To keep the two surfaces from drifting, the skill carries concepts and moves and links to the site for anything dated. A price copied into a skill folder goes stale there.

A test for any feature: would the learner miss it in session ten? If yes, it is a tool. If no, it is a prop for one lesson and should be cheap.

## Not checked

The thread-reading tool here has an activity view listing every timeline item (seen in its schema). Unchecked: whether an agent can find its own thread's id, whether token counts are in that view, and whether an inline page can open the side tab by itself. The session-data idea depends on the first two.
