## What we teach

> It's not about tools or tricks. It's about the base tools: knowing what the model is, what the harness is, what tools are, and how the thing works with the abstracted world it lives in (files, threads, messages). Approached from different angles, from the non-technical to the medium-technical to the highly technical person.
>
> The core thing has to happen from the start: the wow effect. "Oh shit, this is just a sentence I need to say." It is knowing the possibilities, the boundaries and the mechanisms.
>
> Some agent runs proposed ideas like "they have other tools... a dashboard, a progress tracker, a file". Disregard those ideas: they are primitive and behind. They don't understand where we are in cost. Even at really big token counts it's worth creating artifacts to track your work on demand, personalised to you, always. The work you're doing is the innovation. You are not constrained by systems and rules, but by understanding the basics, the different ways, and both the possibilities and the thing you're doing. That's far more powerful than "I have this skill, and this skill is the one for this task." That is looking at the finger pointing at the moon instead of the moon.
>
> The high-arching goal: empowering people to understand how these tools work. From the first seconds of the first lesson we show them they are the most capable, not by saying it but by showing it, by what the lesson suggests. The wow of just installing a skill and kickstarting the experience in a few clicks.

## Getting started (the site)

> The getting-started page is simple: here's Claude, here's OpenAI, click these links and install. Here's T3 Code. Then the "Install the skill" button (placeholder mechanism and link for now). That's it.
>
> Then the fourth step: it's installed, you have it open, this is how it looks, click this button, create a new project thread, we're getting started.

## The first session (the skill)

> When you hit Enter, the skill sets up, and immediately there's an introduction explaining the T3 Code way to use your agents (not a T3 Code product tour). Depending on the level you set at the start, in the chat, you get more and more explanation.
>
> Inline HTML, absolutely: styled, themeable, changeable, adaptable, including how the content is presented to them.
>
> We teach them about the tool, about this skill and what they are doing here, and immediately that they are tailoring this experience to the max. When we teach the AGENTS.md file (the base context), we tell them: "Tell the agent right now to change AGENTS.md to what you want, and let it show you." Careful with non-coders: files are a complication, and T3 Code's right sidebar and side drawer are a lot of complex stuff for them.
>
> The flow: walkthrough, kickstart, set up the tool, explain what's happening. At the end: "Now tell the agent to use this guide skill whenever you want information." Then: "Tell your chat to start a fresh thread, or create a new project folder, and get started."
>
> Teach orchestration: telling one thread to do something in other threads, dragging or copying threads into your chat to reference them fast. This needs the basics first.
>
> At the tool-use part, explain what you are doing and where your work is: your threads, your messages, and the side effects of the agent's messages (files, the system). Threads are just text somewhere; they can be looked up, searched and referenced, depending on the tool. So you can meta-reference your own work.
>
> The core: you are always just a few sentences away from individual thought, through your own logic and orchestrated systems of different agents, not some fixed pipeline. You can encode a flow in a skill, but you can also just freeform it: two sentences of effort for hours of work done for you. You can always just do things ad hoc. The important thing is being focused, working on something, and really understanding what's happening, to whatever degree is necessary.
