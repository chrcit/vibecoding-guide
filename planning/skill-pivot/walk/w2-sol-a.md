## Curriculum review

**L1.** The €85 constraint turned a generic comparison into a visible revision and gave the designer a reason to inspect assumptions. The saved “My next step:” marker made the first fresh reply an observable transfer test. The two distinct approval actions need to stay distinct in the lesson: confirming experience and approving exact file text. The layers lesson also needs a traceable call or an honest account of model arithmetic. The designer could reuse “Which parts of that did you guess?” and the check she composed: “Recalculate each fee per class from the original list, then verify that a total equal to the cap qualifies.”

**L3.** The little umbrella detour and growing workshop gave “one job, one thread” a visible decision point. The most transferable move was retrieving just two values once there were two threads. Include the sidebar action, permission check and guide invocation with the first handover so the learner can act on it. For long threads, the physicist would reuse the guide’s diagnostic about quoting constraints and naming their source, and “Summarise what the next thread needs to know from this one, in one paragraph.” The thread retained the early requirement through the expanded benchmark. An actual compaction event and the UI reference picker were untested.

**L5.** A file the ecologist could recognise, open and change gave the lesson more traction than an explanation of storage alone. The local change audit and the question about an office sheet were good entry points for provider transfer and employer visibility. Place the snapshot offer at the first saved artifact in a returning session, while the learner still has a known-good version. The ecologist would reuse “What did you change on my computer today?”, “Make me invented data with the same columns and kinds of values as mine,” and “Undo just the change in the frog sheet and keep the note.”
