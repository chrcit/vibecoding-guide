This rechecks [the earlier capability notes](../journey-mobile/research-agent-capabilities.md) for [the first two lessons](BRIEF.md). **Verified** means the primary source supports the scoped claim. **Observation** means an attributed case or firsthand account. **Unverified** means the source or scope could not be confirmed. **Wrong** flags a wording or interpretation the source contradicts.

## What to put in the lessons

**Lesson 1, Say what you want.** Use a fictional list and show two possible replies. Without the list or access to it, the model might invent names or ask to see the list. With the list pasted, it can answer from the supplied rows. In the fresh thread, leave out those earlier messages. The project and model settings can still carry over. [OpenAI research on hallucinations](https://openai.com/index/why-language-models-hallucinate/) · [T3 Code thread docs](https://github.com/pingdotgg/t3code/blob/main/docs/user/thread-sidebar.md)

**Lesson 2, It gets hands.** Door check-in is a credible feature-sized request if the app already has tonight’s list and a way to save arrivals. Have the simulated agent search the code, edit it, run a check, see a failure and try again within the same conversation turn. Use fake guests to check that a listed guest can be found and marked in, an unlisted name stays unlisted, and a second tap cannot check the guest in twice. A run at phone width can show the layout and flow. One-handed comfort needs a try on a phone. [Codex prompting](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/prompting) · [Codex agent loop](https://openai.com/index/unrolling-the-codex-agent-loop/) · [T3 Code tool activity](https://github.com/pingdotgg/t3code/blob/main/packages/shared/src/toolActivity.ts)

For the blocked run, the final reply can say what was edited and which check could not run. Leave the missing result visible in the work log. The 8.88% Sol result below can support a source note about this failure mode. The study gives no rate for how often a real Codex user sees an overstated “done.” [OpenAI Blocker Deception evaluation](https://deploymentsafety.openai.com/gpt-6-2-astra-sol/blocker-deception)

### Corrections to the earlier summary

- **Wrong** as written. Later models have published time horizons. The legacy METR page still says “This page is no longer actively updated” and its 16-hour warning applies to its own suite. METR’s **20 November** update runs Fable 5, Fable 5.1, GPT-6 Astra and Opus 5.5 among others. It reports an “estimated time horizon of up to 27 hours” on its verifiable TH2 subset. The appendix’s separate pooled TH1.1 + TH2 fit gives GPT-6 Astra **40 hours** at 50%, with a 95% interval of **13–2,645 hours**. Most new human baselines are researcher estimates. Compare within the same suite, and keep human task time separate from agent runtime. The older 186-minute Mythos Preview result belongs to the legacy 80% series. [Legacy METR series](https://metr.org/time-horizons/) · [November update, §3.A.1 and Appendix B, Figure 26](https://metr.org/blog/2026-11-20-q4-2026-capability-update/)
- **Observation** for the compiler cases. The **about $24,047** figure is the TypeScript-to-Rust author’s API-price equivalent for two weeks of subscription usage. Anthropic’s separate Rust-based C compiler experiment was “just under $20,000” in API costs across two weeks. [TypeScript port account](https://github.com/pingdotgg/ts-rust) · [Anthropic C compiler account](https://www.anthropic.com/research/building-c-compiler)
- **Observation** for Anthropic’s one-line app requests. The Opus 4.5 game-maker runs started from the “same one-sentence prompt”. Solo took 20 minutes and $9 with play mode broken. The planner, builder and evaluator took six hours and $200, with play mode working and bugs remaining. The browser music app was the later Opus 4.6 harness case. Its table gives **3 hr 50 min** and **$124.70**, and one QA pass found that “Audio recording is still stub-only”. [Anthropic harness account](https://www.anthropic.com/research/harness-design-long-running-apps)
- **Verified** as source-specific figures for GPT-6 Astra on Terminal-Bench-Science 0.1. OpenAI’s launch says **64.6%**. Anthropic’s Fable 5.2 card §8.6 attributes **68.1%** at max effort to OpenAI. The older GPT-6 Astra and GPT-6.1 launch tables contain results, but the prior notes retained no specific claim for those tables to validate beyond this discrepancy. ## Keep these figures out of lesson prose

- **The request-size ladder’s minute, hour, file and line ranges.** The sources do not establish the “measured middle” or a typical runtime for a door feature. Lines changed count volume. METR measures task difficulty in human time, and benchmark accuracies apply to their own tasks and scoring rules.
- **22.28%, 23.00%, 4.96% and 6.78% as rates of false “done”.** They come from different setups and denominators. The first two omit Codex’s developer prompt, and the replay rates count several kinds of flagged behavior. Even the 8.88% figure needs its deliberate-blocker setup in the same sentence.
- **“Security pass rates sit near 55%” for agents, 26/30 as a general rate, and 74/43,849 as the risk of using an agent.** Veracode measured selected code-generation tasks without security guidance. DryRun followed changes to two apps. The tracker counts public cases it could attribute, with no denominator of all AI-authored changes. [Veracode study](https://www.veracode.com/blog/spring-2026-genai-code-security/) · [DryRun study](https://www.dryrun.security/the-agentic-coding-security-report) · [Georgia Tech tracker](https://vibesecradar.com/)
- **A numerical Cursor merge rate, a Fable 5.2 false-completion rate, a current SWE-bench Verified leader, and an exact maximum cloud session length.** The sources checked here leave those values unconfirmed.
- **Benchmark scores as interchangeable percentages or as odds of shipping.** Keep Anthropic’s 93.0% and 67.6% attached to its own tests and versions. Vals measures workflows. Artificial Analysis combines distinct benchmarks. Google’s 0.0% cheating and 99.2% honesty figures belong to separate vendor evaluations. Keep disputed Terminal-Bench-Science values and the unqualified Neon first-place claim out of the lesson. [Anthropic system card](https://assets.claude.com/27d60918d218125e7504bc7354c4b4626ec56b11.pdf) · [Vals methods](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.04601) · [Artificial Analysis methodology](https://artificialanalysis.ai/methodology/coding-agents-benchmarking) · [Google Neon launch](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-4-neon/)
- **The marathon and cost anecdotes as defaults, ceilings or budget forecasts.** The 18–52-hour runs, 10,000–151,000-line PRs, eight-day operating system, compiler costs, 90-second demo and runaway fix-loop totals are selected cases. They can illustrate specific later stories with attribution and the conditions of each run. The guide’s own 30–60 minutes belongs to its existing dated observation. [Cursor research preview](https://cursor.com/blog/long-running-agents) · [Anthropic Fable launch](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-fable-and-mythos-5-2) · [Guide observations](../../site/src/data/observations.ts)

No rate was confirmed for truthful “all checks passed” replies in everyday coding, for general unattended completion, or for feature requests that rely on live third-party accounts. The nightclub lessons can show the requested behavior and the evidence a reader would inspect.
